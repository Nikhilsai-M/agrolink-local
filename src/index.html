01 The challenge

What gets lost between
the farm and the table?

Every extra handoff can make it harder for growers and households to see what’s happening along the way.

A smaller share for growers

When produce passes through several intermediaries, farmers may receive a limited share of the final retail price.

Less visibility for households

Shoppers often have little information about where produce was grown, when it was harvested, or how the price was set.

Fragmented demand

Scattered orders make routes harder to plan. When demand is hard to predict and storage is limited, more produce can go unsold.

02 How it works

From harvest to handover.

Our proposed marketplace would bring growers, households, and local collection hubs into the same ordering cycle.

  1. 01

    Farmers list their harvest

    Growers publish what they expect to harvest, when it will be ready, and how much is available.

  2. 02

    Households choose produce

    Residents browse farm-linked listings and plan an individual order or join their community’s order.

  3. 03

    Local hubs group orders

    A collection hub brings produce together, checks and sorts it, then prepares it for dispatch.

  4. 04

    Produce reaches the community

    Households receive produce in a scheduled window. Farmer payments follow the agreed terms.

04 For communities

One shared delivery.
Many dinner tables.

Through their Resident Welfare Association (RWA) or apartment coordinator, neighbours could pool orders for a single scheduled delivery.

  • Your RWA shares a box and a clear order cutoff.
  • Each household chooses how many boxes it needs.
  • Produce arrives at an agreed pickup point in the society.

A shared drop could reduce delivery costs when enough households order nearby. Savings depend on order volume and actual route costs.

08 Common questions

A few things
to know.

About the concept, the planned pilot, and how participation could work.

What is AgroLink Local?

AgroLink Local is a farm-to-community marketplace concept for Bengaluru. It’s designed to link smallholder growers with households through harvest-based listings and planned local deliveries.

How will customers order produce?

The proposed flow lets you browse available produce, see unit prices and growing details, build a basket, and choose a delivery window. You can try an illustrative version in the marketplace preview.

How do community bulk orders work?

A Resident Welfare Association (RWA) or apartment coordinator would share an order and its cutoff with residents. Household quantities would be combined and delivered to a shared handover point. Any delivery-cost benefit depends on local order volume and logistics.

Can customers order individually?

Individual baskets are part of the proposed pilot alongside community orders. Availability, minimum quantities, and delivery fees will be evaluated by locality.

How will produce quality be checked?

The proposed hub process includes checking produce for visible damage and sorting it before dispatch. The pilot will help define handling standards and how to resolve quality concerns.

How will delivery scheduling work?

Available windows would reflect the harvest, route capacity, and community handover arrangements. Residents would see the cutoff and expected window before confirming an order.

How can farmers participate?

Growers can express interest through the farmer inquiry pathway when it opens. Pilot onboarding would cover crops, harvest timing, available volumes, handover, and payment terms.

Is AgroLink Local currently available?

AgroLink Local is at prototype stage. A Bengaluru pilot is planned for apartment communities in HSR Layout and along Sarjapur Road. The marketplace and group order on this site are simulations.

How would the business model work?

The proposed model is a 12% commission on completed produce sales, with potential delivery fees based on logistics. A ₹500 produce order is a planning assumption, with a proposed ₹60 commission component. Optional prepaid boxes are being explored at about ₹500 per week or ₹2,000 for four weekly boxes. The pilot will need to validate these preliminary pricing assumptions before any offer is made.
